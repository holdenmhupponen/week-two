- I found it interesting how Prof. Graham discusses the flaw in assuming that the processing of data is as unbiased and apathetic as the storing of it. It reminds me of the debated role of the documentarian: While they often use the defense of their being the "unbiased arbiter," modern critics point out the necessary bias in constructing any such piece of work. Not every single piece of footage can be shown in the final product; thus, the documentarian must pick and choose what best serves his ideal version of the story. Likewise, Graham connects this thought to the storer of data (including we, the students), because data without a story is mere information, while data with a story is a carefully put together statement.
- In regards to that idea above ^, I see a similar motif in the "Data Feminism" paper. Well, the whole paper is on that essentially. Its discussion on the outdated systems of binaries and hierarchies in regards to gender reminds me of a survey project I did in Grade 11 for my final psychology paper. My research was on the "Diffusion of Responsibility," wherein a group's size is inversely proportionate to the inclination for any individual to act in the case of an emergency. So, I send around surveys. One of the fields was gender, since the original theory did not take into account gender but I wanted to. One problem I found, though, was with the way I created the options: A dropdown box of Man, Woman, Non-binary, or Other (which included a text box). When I looked at the results, I was intrigued by how the gender identities selected sometimes did not reflect the identities by which I knew the people in real life. When I asked them, they reported that, to paraphrase, they "didn't want to be an outlier," and so picked a broader grouping. I.e. a genderfluid identifying person choosing "Non-binary" instead of writing out their preferred identity. I understand now that my issue was creating that survey with an inherent hierarchy: I placed an emphasis on the most prevalent gender identities, reducing an entire categorization of people into an "Other". If I were to do that same survey again, I think I would change the modality entirely into a blank textbox, therefore reducing any such hierarchies or biases.
